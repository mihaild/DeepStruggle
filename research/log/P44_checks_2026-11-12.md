# P44 checks, 2026-11-12 — the league branch is stronger than J1's continuation; step size; who sat where

**Conclusion.**

* **Step 0a's league branch is stronger than J1's own continuation over the same 800M**: 54.7% ± 0.6 head to
  head (8,000 games), 64.2% against 60.9% on the old reference (4,000 games each), 62.3 against 57.6 on R98,
  53.4 against 48.5 on J1's best SWA. It is the first run to move J1's plateau. One branch from one state.
* **In that branch the exploiters played the seat they were never trained in for half their games**, and the
  opponent changed every 128 steps inside a game. Both are how the pool has always worked; neither is what a
  league needs ([P45](../plans/P45_league.md)).
* **The head-norm arm beats the control 55.3% ± 1.1 at 3,200M, directly.** Run-to-run spread at this step is
  about 7 points on the old reference, so this is a lead and not yet a recipe result.
* **J1's policy moves as much per update at 17,000M as at 4,000M** while its head norms double; the L2 arms
  held the heads at fresh norm and kept early-training step sizes; the distil-then-RL runs took 3-6 times
  J1's plateau step at half its rate. The plateau is not heads that cannot move.
* **The reported human games are missing about two thirds of the models' late wins in both seats**, so the
  seat gap read from them (P44) is withdrawn; how people win is unaffected.
* **The old reference still separates.** J1 is at about 61-64% on it; the owner's target is 75% or higher.
  P44's "no longer separates players at J1's level" was this plan's claim and is withdrawn.

## 2. How the pool seated the league members (code, `ai/training/opponent_pool.py` at `dc9b776`)

* `_draw_side` draws the learner's seat 50/50 at each episode end, whoever the opponent is. R108 (trained as
  US only) and R109 (USSR only) therefore played their untrained seat in half of their exposure; P41 measured
  that seat at 3-8% against J1.
* `start_iteration` draws one opponent per iteration (128 steps) for every mixed environment, league or not,
  so a game of ~600 decisions meets about five opponents in sequence. A league member never played a game
  from its first move to its last.
* 0a's league share was `--league-frac 0.5` of the pool's 0.3: 15% of games, 7.5% with a member in its
  trained seat.

P24's *For the next league* (owner, 2026-10-09) already says "played in the main's pool only in that seat";
it was never built.

Mean of `approx_kl_us` and `approx_kl_ussr` (the policy's change over an update, on the batch) and
`head_norm_over_fresh_policy_head`, per bin: every 25th logged iteration for J1, every 10th for the P44 runs,

| R122 (L2 1e-3), 400-465M | 3e-4 | 1.56 | 0.93 |
| R123 (L2 1e-4), 400-528M | 3e-4 | 1.93 | 1.01 |
| D7, `E8-A8-R116-S44`, 0-20M / 120-140M | 1.5e-4 | 2.01 / 1.58 | 2.1 / 2.2 |
| D6, `E8-A30-R115-S44`, 0-75M | 1.5e-4 | 4.2 | 1.7-1.8 |

* J1's step falls by about 40% while its heads grow from 3 to 9 times fresh, then by 15% while they double
  again: at the plateau the policy still moves 0.7e-3 per update and gets nowhere.
* A network with fresh-scale heads takes larger steps at the same rate. The L2 arms never left that regime
  (and read 8.5 / 11.5% at 400M against 18.6%); the distilled students started in it at a level only a small
  step sustains, and lost it (D6 50.8 -> 23.5% in 10M, D7 43.1% against its start at 410M). Arm C, the one
  distilled student run at 5e-5, held.
* **Reading (a hypothesis):** the plateau is where signal and noise balance at the current step, and weight
  growth is a slow decay of that step. It would be overturned by a branch that lowers the step at J1's
  plateau (rate / 3) and gains nothing even raw, or by a distilled student at a matched step (about 5e-5)
  that still loses its start.

## 5. The human games: who sat where, and what is missing

The 183 games P44's table counts (aggregates only; the owner's dump of 2026-11-10). Self-play shares are
2,048 greedy games of each of the two networks: 79% of US wins and 56% of USSR wins end after turn 7.

| whose win | by turn 7 / later | late share | self-play |
|:---|:---|---:|---:|
| model as US | 8 / 10 | 56% | 79% |
| model as USSR | 42 / 20 | 32% | 56% |
| human as USSR | 24 / 25 | 51% | 56% |
| human as US | 11 / 39 | 78% | 79% |

* Human wins are timed as self-play wins are; of the model's late wins about a third appear (10 of ~30 as
  US, 20 of ~53 as USSR). The page has no resign and sends nothing unless a finished game is added, so a
  player who is losing a long game leaves (owner, 2026-11-12). With the late wins at the self-play ratio the
  model scores 43.9% as US and 65.4% as USSR in place of 27.5 and 55.3.
* The seat term fitted on the reported games (model as US -181 Elo, 95% -346 to -31 over people; -77 to -221
  leaving one person out) is therefore mostly this, and P44's "the US seat is the weak one against people" is
  withdrawn. So is "won games are already ahead after turn 1": the reported wins are the quick ones.
* What it does not touch: Europe Control is 9 of the 50 human wins as US (against 1.3-1.4% of US wins in the
  two players' self-play reports) and 3 of the 49 as USSR (5.1-5.5% of USSR wins in self-play). The models'
  USSR loses Europe to people; their US does not lose it more than in self-play.
