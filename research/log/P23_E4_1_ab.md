## Strength at matched steps

`data/reports/e41_matched_2026-09-23.{md,json}`: 9 players, 100 games per side per pair,
temperature 0. The head-to-head is the E4.1 row's win rate **as USSR / as US** against the E4 run
at the same seed and step.

| seed @ step | collapse state of E4.1 | E4.1 Elo | E4 Elo | Δ | head-to-head |
|:---|:---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 3 @ 5M | before | 1513 | 1486 | +28 | 60 / 47 |
| 5 @ 15M | before (at the 10M swing's recovery) | 1831 | 1780 | +51 | 63 / 56 |
| 5 @ 30M | inside | 1857 | 1962 | −105 | 67 / **13** |
| 3 @ 80M | inside since 25M | 1804 | 2164 | −360 | 28 / **3** |

* **Before its collapse, E4.1 is level with E4 or slightly ahead** (+28, +51). With 200 games per
  pair that is within noise, but on the right side of it on both seeds.
* **Inside the collapse, the loss is all on the US seat.** E4.1's US wins 13% and 3% of the
  head-to-heads, while its USSR stays competitive: E4.1-01-03@80M as USSR beats E4-08-05@30M 65%
  of the time.
* So nothing here shows the merged view makes a decision worse. What it may do is make the run
  more collapse-prone, which is 2 of 2 seeds against 0 of 2. Whether that is the view or chance
  is exactly what an A/B under a collapse-robust recipe would settle.

One fact about the view that bears on this: at an op-choice node, the merged view offers every
country slot beside the modes. An untrained policy therefore starts at a higher entropy, 2.75
against 2.08 on seed 5 at 0M.
