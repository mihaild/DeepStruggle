* **Before its collapse, E4.1 is level with E4 or slightly ahead** (+28, +51). With 200 games per

* **Inside the collapse, the loss is all on the US seat.** E4.1's US wins 13% and 3% of the
  head-to-heads, while its USSR stays competitive: E4.1-01-03@80M as USSR beats E4-08-05@30M 65%
  of the time.
* So nothing here shows the merged view makes a decision worse. What it may do is make the run
  more collapse-prone, which is 2 of 2 seeds against 0 of 2. Whether that is the view or chance
  is exactly what an A/B under a collapse-robust recipe would settle.

One fact about the view that bears on this: at an op-choice node, the merged view offers every
country slot beside the modes. An untrained policy therefore starts at a higher entropy, 2.75
against 2.08 on seed 5 at 0M.

## Rerun on the clean recipe: E4.1-01-36 (2026-09-24)

E4-08-36's flags plus `--merged-influence`, seed 36, from scratch to 160M in one run. The code is
current: the fixed opponent pool, the fixed CUDA graphs, and the clang engine. `launch_flags.py
--diff` against E4-08-36 shows `--merged-influence` and `--train-steps` only. Same-seed baseline:
E4-08-36 ([`E4_clean_replicates.md`](E4_clean_replicates.md)).

**It collapsed again, on the US seat, early and for about 100M steps.** Self-play USSR share by
5M:

The run was pinned for most of 35–130M.
* The US seat's entropy never came down: 2.49 at 0–5M, 2.26–2.77 through the episode. E4-08-36's
  US seat sat at 1.6–1.8 over the same stretch.
* During the pin the US seat's KL fell to about half the USSR's (0.010 against 0.018–0.023).
* The episode ended when the US seat started moving: KL 0.086, entropy down to 1.55 at 140M.

**Reading.**
* E4.1 was level with E4 before collapsing (−36 at 40M), as in the first A/B.
* The collapse cost more than a delay: E4.1 regressed from 40M to 80M, gained nothing until the US
  seat moved at about 130M, and ended 300 behind.
* E4.1 has now collapsed early in **3 of 3** runs (seeds 3, 5, 36). The E4 view does so on about 1
  seed in 6 ([`E4_collapse_is_recoverable.md`](E4_collapse_is_recoverable.md)).
* The consistent sign is the losing seat's entropy staying high. The merged view's larger action
  space starts higher (2.75 against 2.08), and under a fixed entropy bonus the US seat never
  sharpens.

As it stands E4.1 is not usable. The candidate repair targets that entropy: the one-sided per-seat
ceiling (`--entropy-ceiling`, P25 3k, implemented), or an entropy coefficient scaled by the
starting-entropy ratio (0.01 × 2.08 / 2.75 ≈ 0.0076).

## A lower entropy coefficient on both views (E4.1-02-36, E4-54-36), 2026-09-24

The entropy coefficient was scaled by the starting-entropy ratio, 0.01 × 2.08 / 2.75 ≈ 0.0076.
It is symmetric: both seats get the same coefficient. Both runs used seed 36 and ran to 160M:

* **On E4 the lower coefficient is neutral:** +56, +13, +27 and −45 against 0.01 at 40–160M,
  inside a single seed's noise. Its seats sharpen a little further (entropy 1.25 / 1.01 at the
  end, against 1.21 / 1.15 for E4-08-36 at 240M), with no collapse (peak 0.75).
* **On E4.1 it does not prevent the collapse, and changes its shape.** E4.1-02-36 reached 0.95 by
  25–30M, about when E4.1-01-36 did. My early readout said the lower coefficient delayed the pin by

  E4.1-01-36's US seat started relearning at about 130M.

  entropy bonus is not what keeps it high.**

  as USSR and 6% as US. E4.1's USSR play is roughly on par with E4's; its US play never develops.

**Reading.** E4.1 has now collapsed on the US seat in **4 of 4** runs, at either entropy
coefficient. A US-only failure that does not respond to the entropy bonus points to something the
merged view does to the US seat's decisions. Candidates:
* the US setup and influence placement, where the merged view composes two steps into one action;
* composed actions whose legality or credit differs by seat.

The next step is diagnosis rather than another lever: per-decision-type entropy and choice
distributions for the US seat in E4.1 against E4, at matched steps.

## Where the US seat goes flat: options and policy spread by decision (2026-09-24)

`ai/eval/decision_entropy.py` plays a checkpoint against itself in its own view (64 games × 400
decisions, temperature 1, CPU). Split by seat, phase and decision type, it records:
* the legal count;
* the entropy as a fraction of log(legal), where 0 is certain and 1 is uniform;
* the top choice's probability.

Raw output: `data/reports/decision_entropy_seed36.{md,json}`. Entries below are
legal / entropy fraction / top p.

* **The opening favours the USSR in both views, as the owner's hypothesis expects.** The US
  places its setup among about 14 countries and the USSR among 9–11. The USSR's placement is
  settled by 10M (0.07 in E4) while the US's is still spread (0.74), so every run opens with the
  USSR ahead (self-play share 0.55–0.65).
* **What separates E4.1 from E4 is the size of the play-mode and op-mode decisions.** They grow
  from about 3–4 options to about 45–55, for both seats. The US has only about 10% more options
  there than the USSR (52 against 47).
* **At 10M both seats spread that decision alike (0.74). The US then flattens** (0.90 at 20M,
  0.96 at 40M, top choice 9%) while the USSR sharpens (0.60). This is the collapse window. E4's
  US seat sharpens steadily over the same stretch.
* E4.1-01-36@160M, after its US seat recovered, is sharp again: US op mode 0.19 / 0.70.

**Reading (inference).** The USSR's opening lead puts the US behind. Its advantage signal fades,
and the fixed entropy bonus acts almost alone. On a 50-option decision the bonus has much more
room: log 50 ≈ 3.9 nats against log 4.4 ≈ 1.5 in E4. So the losing seat drifts to near-uniform
and cannot climb out. Scaling the coefficient by 0.76 (E4.1-02) did not help, which fits a problem
of room rather than scale. Two symmetric tests follow:
* an entropy bonus normalised by log(legal) per decision;
* E4.1 warm-started from an E4 checkpoint that has already learned the opening.
