# P23 stage 5: the E4.1 A/B from scratch

**2026-09-23. Paused, and no verdict on the merits.** Both E4.1 arms collapsed early, and the
comparison is decided by those collapses rather than by the action view. Work moves to collapse
prevention on E4 ([`../plans/P25_collapse_robustness.md`](../archive/E4_ladder/plans/P25_collapse_robustness.md)).
The A/B is to be re-run with whatever recipe P25 adopts.

The plan: [`../plans/P23_merged_influence_E4_1.md`](../plans/P23_merged_influence_E4_1.md). Each
arm is M2d from scratch, and its flags differ from the E4 run on the same seed only in
`--merged-influence` (`launch_flags.py --diff`). The adoption rule is: not worse than E4-08-0s at
matched steps on either seed, per seat.

In the 26-player full field ([`E4_late_dynamics.md`](E4_late_dynamics.md), last section),
E4.1-01-03@80M rates **−259** against E4-08-03@80M. The field is different, so the gap is not
−360 as in the 9-player round above.

* **Before its collapse, E4.1 is level with E4 or slightly ahead** (+28, +51). With 200 games per
  pair that is within noise, but on the right side of it on both seeds.
* **Inside the collapse, the loss is all on the US seat.** E4.1's US wins 13% and 3% of the
  head-to-heads, while its USSR stays competitive: E4.1-01-03@80M as USSR beats E4-08-05@30M 65%
  of the time.
* So nothing here shows the merged view makes a decision worse. What it may do is make the run
  more collapse-prone, which is 2 of 2 seeds against 0 of 2. Whether that is the view or chance
  is exactly what an A/B under a collapse-robust recipe would settle.

One fact about the view that bears on this: at an op-choice node, the merged view offers every
country slot beside the modes. An untrained policy therefore starts at a higher entropy, 2.75
against 2.08 on seed 5 at 0M.

## Rerun on the clean recipe: E4.1-01-36 (2026-09-24)

E4-08-36's flags plus `--merged-influence`, seed 36, from scratch to 160M in one run. The code is
current: the fixed opponent pool, the fixed CUDA graphs, and the clang engine. `launch_flags.py
--diff` against E4-08-36 shows `--merged-influence` and `--train-steps` only. Same-seed baseline:
E4-08-36 ([`E4_clean_replicates.md`](E4_clean_replicates.md)).

**It collapsed again, on the US seat, early and for about 100M steps.** Self-play USSR share by
5M:

.55 .55 .74 .88 .82 .90 .90 .97 .97 .98 .97 .91 .93 .99 .97 .97 .99 .98 .98 .96 .98 .95 .98 .97 .98 .98 .92 .93 .93 .84 .81 .84

The run was pinned for most of 35–130M.
* The US seat's entropy never came down: 2.49 at 0–5M, 2.26–2.77 through the episode. E4-08-36's
  US seat sat at 1.6–1.8 over the same stretch.
* During the pin the US seat's KL fell to about half the USSR's (0.010 against 0.018–0.023).
* The episode ended when the US seat started moving: KL 0.086, entropy down to 1.55 at 140M.

Strength, from `data/reports/e4.1-01-36_160M.{md,json}` (12 players, 100 games per side per pair):

|:---|---:|---:|---:|
| 40M | 1791 | 1827 | −36 |

At 160M against E4-08-36@160M, E4.1 wins 25% as USSR and 6% as US.

**Reading.**
* E4.1 was level with E4 before collapsing (−36 at 40M), as in the first A/B.
* The collapse cost more than a delay: E4.1 regressed from 40M to 80M, gained nothing until the US
  seat moved at about 130M, and ended 300 behind.
* E4.1 has now collapsed early in **3 of 3** runs (seeds 3, 5, 36). The E4 view does so on about 1
  seed in 6 ([`E4_collapse_is_recoverable.md`](E4_collapse_is_recoverable.md)).
* The consistent sign is the losing seat's entropy staying high. The merged view's larger action
  space starts higher (2.75 against 2.08), and under a fixed entropy bonus the US seat never
  sharpens.

As it stands E4.1 is not usable. The candidate repair targets that entropy: the one-sided per-seat
ceiling (`--entropy-ceiling`, P25 3k, implemented), or an entropy coefficient scaled by the
starting-entropy ratio (0.01 × 2.08 / 2.75 ≈ 0.0076).

## A lower entropy coefficient on both views (E4.1-02-36, E4-54-36), 2026-09-24

The entropy coefficient was scaled by the starting-entropy ratio, 0.01 × 2.08 / 2.75 ≈ 0.0076.
It is symmetric: both seats get the same coefficient. Both runs used seed 36 and ran to 160M:
* **E4.1-02-36:** E4.1-01-36's flags plus `--entropy-coef 0.0076`;
* **E4-54-36:** E4-08-36's flags plus `--entropy-coef 0.0076`, the control.

  end, against 1.21 / 1.15 for E4-08-36 at 240M), with no collapse (peak 0.75).
* **On E4.1 it does not prevent the collapse, and changes its shape.** E4.1-02-36 reached 0.95 by
  25–30M, about when E4.1-01-36 did. My early readout said the lower coefficient delayed the pin by
  10–15M; the 5M buckets show it did not. It then stayed at 0.91–1.00 to 160M and never recovered.
  E4.1-01-36's US seat started relearning at about 130M.
* The US seat's entropy stayed at 2.5–2.7 at either coefficient, against E4's 1.2–1.7. **The
  entropy bonus is not what keeps it high.**
* **The damage is on the US seat alone.** Against E4-08-36@160M, E4.1-02-36@160M wins 42% as
  USSR, about what E4 itself manages (E4-54-36@160M: 48%), but 2% as US. E4.1-01-36@160M wins 25%
  as USSR and 6% as US. E4.1's USSR play is roughly on par with E4's; its US play never develops.

**Reading.** E4.1 has now collapsed on the US seat in **4 of 4** runs, at either entropy
coefficient. A US-only failure that does not respond to the entropy bonus points to something the
merged view does to the US seat's decisions. Candidates:
* the US setup and influence placement, where the merged view composes two steps into one action;
* composed actions whose legality or credit differs by seat.

The next step is diagnosis rather than another lever: per-decision-type entropy and choice
distributions for the US seat in E4.1 against E4, at matched steps.

## Where the US seat goes flat: options and policy spread by decision (2026-09-24)

`ai/eval/decision_entropy.py` plays a checkpoint against itself in its own view (64 games × 400
decisions, temperature 1, CPU). Split by seat, phase and decision type, it records:
* the legal count;
* the entropy as a fraction of log(legal), where 0 is certain and 1 is uniform;
* the top choice's probability.

Raw output: `data/reports/decision_entropy_seed36.{md,json}`. Entries below are
legal / entropy fraction / top p.

| USSR setup placement | 11.4 / 0.07 / 0.94 | 13.4 / 0.12 / 0.90 | 9.6 / 0.37 / 0.71 | 9.7 / 0.35 / 0.72 | 11.1 / 0.13 / 0.90 |
| US play mode | 4.4 / 0.87 / 0.43 | 4.2 / 0.53 / 0.69 | 51.6 / 0.74 / 0.27 | 52.6 / 0.90 / 0.14 | 52.8 / 0.96 / 0.09 |
| USSR play mode | 4.5 / 0.64 / 0.59 | 4.5 / 0.60 / 0.64 | 45.4 / 0.74 / 0.27 | 48.2 / 0.71 / 0.30 | 47.4 / 0.60 / 0.42 |
| US op mode | 3.0 / 0.86 / 0.54 | 3.0 / 0.51 / 0.76 | 43.9 / 0.61 / 0.47 | 42.3 / 0.82 / 0.26 | 46.5 / 0.96 / 0.09 |

* **The opening favours the USSR in both views, as the owner's hypothesis expects.** The US
  places its setup among about 14 countries and the USSR among 9–11. The USSR's placement is
  settled by 10M (0.07 in E4) while the US's is still spread (0.74), so every run opens with the
  USSR ahead (self-play share 0.55–0.65).
* **What separates E4.1 from E4 is the size of the play-mode and op-mode decisions.** They grow
  from about 3–4 options to about 45–55, for both seats. The US has only about 10% more options
  there than the USSR (52 against 47).
* **At 10M both seats spread that decision alike (0.74). The US then flattens** (0.90 at 20M,
  0.96 at 40M, top choice 9%) while the USSR sharpens (0.60). This is the collapse window. E4's
  US seat sharpens steadily over the same stretch.
* E4.1-01-36@160M, after its US seat recovered, is sharp again: US op mode 0.19 / 0.70.

**Reading (inference).** The USSR's opening lead puts the US behind. Its advantage signal fades,
and the fixed entropy bonus acts almost alone. On a 50-option decision the bonus has much more
room: log 50 ≈ 3.9 nats against log 4.4 ≈ 1.5 in E4. So the losing seat drifts to near-uniform
and cannot climb out. Scaling the coefficient by 0.76 (E4.1-02) did not help, which fits a problem
of room rather than scale. Two symmetric tests follow:
* an entropy bonus normalised by log(legal) per decision;
* E4.1 warm-started from an E4 checkpoint that has already learned the opening.

## A distilled warm start: E4-08-36@80M translated into E4.1 (2026-09-24)

A raw E4 network loaded into the E4.1 view puts all of its op-choice mass on influence (KL 17.6
to the policy it implies). So E4.1-05-36 starts from a distillation with exact action
translation:
* `tools/lib/merged_targets.py`: P(influence, first point X) = P_E4(influence) × P_E4(X | after
  the commit), every other action unchanged;
* `tools/generate_policy_targets.py --merged-view`: the teacher plays in the E4.1 view and records
  the translated policy;
* `--mode distill`, which replays in the E4.1 view because the sidecar says so.

**The dataset:** `data/datasets/e4.1_distill_E4-08-36_80M_mix.jsonl.gz`, 4,000 games and 721,198
targets. It has 2,000 games at temperature 1.0 and 500 at each rollout band (0.15, 0.50, 0.10,
0.35).

Building it turned up one bug, fixed in `c54e667`. Where committing ops to influence ends the
game, there is no second step, and asking the teacher at the terminal post-commit state gave a
NaN target. Such rows now keep the whole commit.

**How close the student is, 6 epochs at LR 1e-4** (`tools/scripts/merged_view_warmstart.py
--student`, 128 games):

| | raw E4 net in the E4.1 view | distilled, 2 epochs | distilled, 6 epochs |
|:---|---:|---:|---:|
| KL(translated ‖ model) at op-choice, mean | 17.57 | 0.074 | 0.044 |
| influence mass (implied 0.334) | 1.000 | 0.325 | 0.343 |
| top-1 agreement | 18.0% | 89.6% | 91.9% |

At temperature 0 the student rates about 110 below its teacher (1879 against 1992;
`data/reports/e4.1_distilled6_vs_teacher.*`), and 6 epochs did not close the gap. By seat, the
teacher wins 53% as USSR against the student but 78% as US, so the loss is on the student's USSR
seat.

This is at least partly built in. At temperature 0, greedy play over the composed E4.1 action is
not greedy play over E4's two steps: influence spread over several countries can lose the argmax
to a single alternative mode that E4's greedy first step would not pick.

**E4.1-05-36:** from the 6-epoch student, the E4-08 recipe (λ 0.98, pool 0.3/12) with
`--merged-influence`, seed 36, 80M. That makes it comparable to E4-08-36's 80–160M. It is a warm
start, so the optimizer, π_ref and the opponent pool are fresh, unlike E4-08-36's own
continuation.

**Result: E4.1-05-36 pinned by 20M and was stopped.** The USSR share of self-play went from
0.30 (0–2M) to 0.66 (4–10M) and then to 0.92–0.98 (12–20M). The US seat's entropy rose from
1.07 to 2.78 over those 20M, and it started rising while the US seat was still *winning*.
`ai.eval.decision_entropy` shows the US policy going almost uniform on every decision type,
not just the merged op-choice. The USSR seat stays about where the teacher left it.

**The warm start itself dissolves the US seat, in both views.** In the E4 view the US
policy's entropy fraction goes from 0.06–0.47 to 0.71–0.77 within the first 5M. It then stays
there, while the uninterrupted continuation from the same weights stays at 0.11–0.50. The E4
self-play share holds near 0.6 for 12M and then drifts to 0.84 by 20M. So the E4 control is
slower and stops short of uniform, but it goes the same way.

E4.1 makes it worse rather than causing it. The US fraction there reaches 0.98–0.99 on every
decision type, and the pin arrives at about 12M instead of starting at about 16M.

An earlier reading of this control from the training log alone (the 10–15M window, share 0.62,
US entropy 1.53) concluded that the reset was not the cause. The probe and the last 5M
contradict that.

This bears on the from-scratch E4.1 collapses only in part. A from-scratch run has no sharp
policy to lose, so a reset effect cannot explain them. What the two share is the seat: in every
case the US seat goes flat and the USSR seat does not.

A warm start differs from a resume in three things. A resume restores the optimizer's Adam
moments, π_ref and the opponent pool (`load_resume_state`); a warm start begins with fresh Adam
moments, π_ref equal to the starting weights, and an empty pool. Which of these dissolves the US
seat is not yet tested.
