# One night's measurements, 2026-09-18 — and seven things that turned out to be wrong

An index for the session, in the order the results arrived, with each claim at the confidence it
actually earned. Every number here is recorded in full somewhere else; nothing in this file is the
only copy.

## What was wrong, and corrected in the same session

This is the more useful list.

1. **"The collapse was an artifact."** Too strong. `E3-35-28` declines at ~30M with a
   demonstrably healthy pool — 73.6% → 58.2% — carrying the documented signature. Starvation
   **accelerated and deepened** a real failure mode rather than inventing it. The healthy-pool
   decline also *plateaus* at ~58% instead of falling to 24%, so the pool bounds it.
2. **"The KL regulariser crowds the CE term out of the update."** Refuted by the same arm.
   Iterations with `kl_div` > 1.0 average `grad_frac` 0.1166 against 0.1339 when it is low — no
   effect, wrong direction — and `grad_frac` fell to its lowest value *after* the KL returned to
   below its original baseline.
3. **"The treatment at 70M exceeds the control's best over 240M."** Not established. The same
   pair reads 33.3% on a second sample against 44.5% on the first; these percentages carry
   **±4–5 pp** and a 6-point gap does not survive that.
4. **Two dense arms ran concurrently for ~90 minutes**, byte-identical, each holding 8.7 GB and
   halving the other's throughput, because a launch script ran twice with nothing to notice.
5. **"The ending mix is why the treatment is stronger."** Both replicate, but they do not track:
   seed B reduces DEFCON-1 endings *more* than seed A (29.2% against 32.6%, self-inflicted 6.25%
   against 12.6%) and gains *less* at every matched point. The ending mix is **what the bands
   do**, reliably; it is not what separates a strong seed from a weaker one.
6. **"The temperature arm buys the seat the control never learns."** The seat asymmetry flipped
   between seeds — A was US-leaning (21.0 / 10.0), B is USSR-leaning (5.0 / 13.0) — so it is a
   property of a *seed*, not of the treatment.

7. **"The CE feedback loop is dead."** Retracted. That rested on target entropy being flat,
   measured over a window that ended ~0.5M steps before the flattening began. Extended to 32.3M
   the targets flatten **+18%**. The loop is still not the *initiator* — the ordering rules that
   out — but it was wrong to call it dead, and the error was a window boundary, not the analysis.

Each of these was caught by running the check that could falsify the claim rather than the one
that would confirm it. The pattern is worth more than any single item on the list above — and
item 7 is the sharpest lesson in it: a null result is only as wide as the window it was measured
over, and this one was reported without checking whether the window covered the whole decline.

## The open question, sharper than it was

**Why does only the USSR seat collapse?** This is now the sharpest form of the question, and it is
sharp because **four** candidate answers have been measured and excluded:

* *not the opponent pool* — capacity 12, span 30M, opponent win rate 0.45–0.49 throughout;
* *not target quality* — both seats' targets degrade by the same ~16% and only one seat falls;
* *not the KL* — `grad_frac` is unrelated to `kl_div` and kept falling after the KL normalised;
* *not determinization* — the USSR/US target-diffuseness ratio is 1.13–1.27 **with perfect
  information too**, slightly larger than with determinization, so hidden-state sampling does not
  create it.

**No proposed mechanism is left standing**, which is a better position than holding a wrong one.
What survives is a *fact about the game rather than about training*: at USSR decisions the search
genuinely finds more moves of near-equal value. That is constant across the whole window, so it
cannot be what takes the seat from 71% to 26% — at most it says which seat sits closer to the
edge.

A second, narrower question rides along: **why does `search_ce_grad_frac` fall 0.238 → 0.025** when
the policy is drifting *further* from its targets, which should make (π − p_target) larger? The
candidates are the other terms' gradients growing or the searched-row count falling, and both are
now instrumented (`search_ce_grad_norm`, `total_grad_norm`, `search_ce_rows`), so the next search
arm answers it rather than another argument.

## Left for the owner

* **The KL covers the opponent's decision rows** while the surrogate and entropy bonus are both
  restricted to `b_learner`. The comment beside `own_entropy` gives the argument for why that is
  wrong for entropy and it applies unchanged to a KL penalty. Changing it changes the objective.
* **Changing the default rollout bands** now has two-seed support and one missing arm. Both treatment seeds beat the control at every point from 40M to 60M, and seed B entered the pre-registered confirm band at 50M and 60M. What is still absent is a **matched control at a second seed**: with one control seed, nothing measured separates the treatment from between-seed variation in the control. That arm, not more points on these two, is what would settle the default.
