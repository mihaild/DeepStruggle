# Paper Review: Off-Belief Learning (OBL)

**Title**: *Off-Belief Learning for imperfect information games*  
**Authors**: Hengyuan Hu, Adam Lerer, Brandon Cui, Luis Pineda, Noam Brown, Jakob Foerster  
**Publication**: *ICML* 2021 / *NeurIPS* 2021

---

## 1. Core Problem Addressed
In imperfect-information games trained with standard self-play, agents frequently develop **brittle, self-reinforcing conventions** (arbitrary action sequences that have specific coded meanings within a single training run). When evaluated against humans or different bots, these conventions break down, leading to severe strategic fragility.

---

## 2. Key Algorithmic Innovations
1. **Off-Belief Learning (OBL)**:
   - Evaluates state beliefs assuming past actions were generated by a fixed baseline policy $\pi_0$ (e.g. uniform random or anchor), while future actions are taken by the optimized policy $\pi_1$.
   - This forces the agent to learn actions that are **grounded in the objective game dynamics** rather than relying on brittle private conventions.
2. **Hierarchical Level-$k$ Reasoning**:
   - Extends OBL into a hierarchy where the level-$(k-1)$ policy serves as the belief prior for the level-$k$ policy, producing grounded, human-compatible strategic reasoning.

---

## 3. Key Takeaways & Relevance for Twilight Struggle
- In Twilight Struggle self-play, agents must not develop arbitrary "convention plays" that fail when playing against human Grandmasters or different bot archetypes.
- Grounding beliefs in objective deck distributions ensures robust play against any opponent.
